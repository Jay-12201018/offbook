# Author notes — Month 1 scenes

Written for an adult Chinese learner who can follow slower familiar talk, loses the utterance when speech speeds up, and often freezes or sounds dry when it's his turn. Month 1 stays inside daily chat and travel. All dialogue is original General American. Nothing here is quoted or lightly rewritten from a show, film, or podcast.

## Voices

**Maya** is warm and a little quick. She notices unfairness (especially around money), softens it with humor, and will practice a hard line in her head before she says it. She is not shy about *I'm serious* or *Don't make it weird*. When she is late or has to send food back, she over-apologizes; that is useful language, not a character flaw to mock.

**Chris** is easygoing and slightly slipperier with plans. He reaches for the check out of habit, says *You're fine* a lot, and needs to be pinned to a real time (*Don't leave your house at seven*). He is not careless with other people's feelings; he is just the person who would rather not do math on the sidewalk.

**Jordan** appears as a third friend, not a comic sidekick. Tired, practical, allergic to spreadsheets at the table. Pronouns are they/them in the scripts, so lines stay *Jordan just texted, they're stuck at work* and *I'll text 'em*. If a recording session needs a binary voice, either is fine; keep the wording.

**Service people** should sound like people at the end of a shift, not like training manuals. Anita will not oversell the pastries. Luis offers to turn the music down. Jess says *I'm not gonna lie*. Omar will not promise forty minutes if it might be an hour. If a line could appear under *How may I help you?* in a textbook, it does not belong here.

## Recurring world

The same small geography keeps coming back on purpose: a cafe, a bookstore cafe, Third Street, a place with a yellow sign, Grant Street (which is always packed), the main station versus the little stop on Fifth, a hotel where Maya checks in under Chen. Money language from Pack 1 (*throw in*, *I got this*, *next one's on me*) returns in the 180 wpm diagnostic and in Pack 3 when a cab is split. *How long are we talking?* moves from Pack 2 into the kitchen delay in Pack 4. That repetition is the pedagogy, not a lack of ideas.

## Register

Daily and travel scenes use discourse markers, reductions, and unfinished sentences, but they stay cooperative and followable. The two TV-register scenes in Pack 4 (*We could just share*, *This is not what I ordered*) add overlap, dry asides, *yeah no*, and a little more attitude, still on the same food-and-help themes. They are not sitcom plots and should not be played as punchline machines. If a take starts to sound like a specific show, throw it out and do it smaller.

## Scene-by-scene doubts

- **diag-listen-120.** I kept function words more complete than in the later diagnostics, then had to trim for length, which brought a few contractions back (*I'm*, *I'll*, *don't*). It should still feel slower and clearer than 150, not robotic. If a recording pass can restore a couple of full *want to* / *I will* forms without going stiff, do that.
- **diag-listen-180.** Three speakers and dense reductions on a first-open diagnostic may overshoot some learners. That is partly the point of the ladder: the task is gist, not a perfect transcript. If testers bounce off it completely, cut Jordan's shoe joke first, not the plan-locking lines.
- **diag-speak-this-week.** The model monologue is deliberately ordinary. Pasta night is not supposed to be impressive. If the model sounds more fluent than a shy learner can stand, that is all right; the prompt tells them they can start with *This week was pretty normal*.
- **pack01-04-card-machine-is-down.** Adds a third voice and denser reductions on the same day the lever is "reduction density," not "more people." I kept Matt's turns short so the extra speaker is color, not a second difficulty jump.
- **pack01-07-split-it-three-ways.** Three-way money talk gets factual (*the drinks were like forty*). If it starts to feel like accounting class, lean on Jordan's *I'm not gonna fight you in a restaurant* and get to the send-the-money close faster.
- **pack04-04-im-allergic-to.** This one has to stay careful. Maya says *actually allergic, not just "I don't love them."* Sam checks with the kitchen, rules out the stir fry, offers a clean pan, and tells her not to touch a plate if she's unsure. I would rather this scene be slightly plainer than clever. If anything in the kitchen explanation sounds sloppy in rehearsal, prefer a clearer "skip the stir fry, grilled fish is fine."
- **pack04-06 and pack04-07 (TV-register).** These are the lines I would listen to most carefully in the booth. The teasing should stay affectionate, not mean. *You're so lying right now* and *I'm not trying to be a jerk* are attitude, not catchphrases. Watch that Chris does not become a sitcom boyfriend, and that Jess does not become a saint or a punchline.
- **Spoken forms.** I wrote reduced forms into `spoken` wherever a General American speaker would naturally reduce them, and often into `text` too when the spelling is lexicalized (*wanna*, *gonna*, *kinda*). Glue items always include a standard expansion. A few reductions are invitational, not mandatory, in production: *didja*, *d'you*, *'em*, *lemme*, *gimme*. If an actor's own speech doesn't land *didja* on that line, a lighter *did you* with a weak *you* is still useful; do not fake a cartoon reduction.

## Recording reminders

Normal speed is the default. The `slowerWpm` field is the fall-back when a first listen fails, not the version people should live in. Allow small hesitations and self-corrections already written into the lines; do not iron them out. Do not add catchphrases, product names of TV shows, or extra cultural jokes that become the price of admission.
